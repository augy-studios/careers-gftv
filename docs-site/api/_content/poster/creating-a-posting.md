# Creating a posting

**Job postings** in the sidebar, then **New posting**. A new posting starts as
a draft, and a draft is not on the board. Nothing you type is public until you
publish it.

![The posting editor](pending:poster-editor-desktop-light "The editor, with the language tabs above the fields.")

## Three things before it can go out

A posting cannot be published without all three. The editor says which are
missing, and so does the postings list.

1. **A title.**
2. **At least one tag**, because tags are how somebody filters the board.
3. **An application form**, because Apply has to lead somewhere.

Everything else is optional. A posting with only these three is a thin posting,
and it is a publishable one.

## The words

The first tab is the posting itself. Every other tab is a translation of it.
See [Writing in more than one
language](/staff/poster/more-than-one-language).

- **Title.** What appears on the card and at the top of the page.
- **Summary.** One or two lines. It is the card's description and the first
  thing most people read.
- **About the role.** The main body.
- **What you would do.** The responsibilities.
- **What we need from you.** The requirements.
- **Nice to have.** Optional extras, and it reads as optional.
- **Location.** Free text. A city, a studio, or something like "anywhere".
- **Note about pay**, which is explained below.

A small amount of formatting is understood in the long fields: paragraphs,
bullet points, bold, and links. Nothing else. A `#` heading is not a heading
here. Use [extra sections](/staff/poster/sections) for that.

## Paid or unpaid

**Every role is unpaid unless This role is paid is ticked.** That is a fact
stored against the posting, and the card and the page both say which.

It is stored per posting on purpose. A sentence in the site copy saying "most
roles are unpaid" ages badly and nobody remembers to change it.

**Note about pay** is where an honest detail goes: a stipend, expenses covered,
or that a paid role is subject to something. Leave it empty when there is
nothing to say.

## Commitment

One of five, and a posting need not state one:

Full time, Part time, Volunteer, Contract, Internship.

The board filters by this, so it is worth setting. **Full time is about hours
and not about money.** A full time volunteer role is a real thing here, and the
paid tickbox is what says otherwise.

## Openings and the closing date

**Openings** is how many people you are looking for. It does not close anything
by itself. Accepting somebody does not reduce it, and nothing counts down.

**Closing date** is optional.

- With one, the overnight pass closes the posting the day after it passes. It
  stops accepting applications and stays readable.
- With none, it stays open until somebody closes it. The board shows those as
  **Open until filled**.

A posting with no closing date is not a mistake. It is a choice, and the
overview counts them separately so a forgotten one is visible.

## Tags

Type to find a tag. Type something that matches nothing and you are offered to
create it.

**Choose the tags somebody would filter by**, and not every word that describes
the role. Three good tags beat nine vague ones. A tag nobody filters by is
noise on every card that carries it.

Before creating a new tag, look for one that already means the same thing. Two
tags meaning one thing split a search in half. See [Managing
tags](/staff/poster/tags).

## The web address

The **Web address** field is the slug: the readable part of the link.

**It is an alias only.** The real address of a posting is its id, so changing
the slug breaks nothing and no shared link dies. Set it to something short and
obvious, or leave what the title gave you.

## Team

Which team the role sits in. It appears on every card and in the search
filters.

If the team you need is not listed, it may not have a name in every language
yet, which is what keeps a team hidden. See [Managing
teams](/staff/poster/teams).

## Questions asked of everybody who applies

Optional, up to twenty. Anybody who applies from now on is asked these as a
separate task, in the portal, on top of the Google Form.

Use them for something you need in a fixed shape and cannot get from a form.
Most postings need none.

**Editing them changes only what the next applicant is asked.** Answers already
given stay attached to the questions that were actually asked.

## Saving, previewing, publishing

**Save** keeps a draft. Save often; nothing is kept in the browser.

**Preview in a new tab** opens the posting as a reader sees it. Save once
first, because a posting has no address until it exists. If the tab does not
open, your browser blocked it.

**Publish** puts it on the board, and it is refused with a reason when one of
the three requirements is missing.

The rest of the life of a posting is [The posting
lifecycle](/staff/poster/the-posting-lifecycle).
