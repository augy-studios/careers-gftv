# Reading the analytics

**Analytics** in the sidebar. How each posting is doing: who opened it, who
clicked Apply, and how many of those told us they went through with it.

![The analytics table](pending:poster-analytics-desktop-light "The analytics table, sorted by apply clicks.")

## The columns

| Column | What it counts |
|---|---|
| Opened | Times the posting page was opened |
| Apply clicks | Times somebody pressed Apply |
| Told us yes | Applications recorded as submitted |
| Conversion | Told us yes, as a share of apply clicks |
| Rating | What applicants thought of the posting |

Sort by any of them, in either direction.

## The conversion rate is a floor

> [!NOTE]
> A click counts as applied only when somebody told us so. Nobody is chased for
> an answer, so the conversion rate is the lowest the truth can be, and never
> an estimate of it.

Three things end a click without a yes, and only one of them is a problem:

- **Told us no.** They looked at the form and did not apply.
- **Never answered.** They have not been back to the site since.
- **Timed out.** The question went unanswered long enough that the overnight
  pass gave up on it.

The last two are silence, and silence is not a no. On a posting with confirmed
submissions set up, most of that silence disappears, because the form reports
for itself.

## Where each yes came from

The detail view splits them three ways:

- **Confirmed by the form itself.** The strongest, and what confirmed
  submissions buys you.
- **The applicant told us.** They answered the question on their next visit.
- **Recorded by staff.** An admin matched an unmatched submission to an
  account.

A posting whose yeses are all self reported is one to set up properly. See
[Turning on confirmed
submissions](/staff/poster/confirmed-submissions).

## Check the form

Some rows carry a **Check the form** flag. It appears when at least five people
clicked Apply and fewer than a fifth of them said they applied.

**That usually means a broken or closed application form, and not a badly
written posting.** Open the form yourself and try to submit it.

Both numbers are deliberately generous. A false flag costs you one look at a
form that turns out to be fine. A missed one costs every applicant who tried.

## Ratings

Applicants can rate a posting when they apply. It is a rating of the posting,
and not of the role or of you.

**An average is only shown from three ratings onwards.** Below that the count
is shown on its own, so one opinion does not read as a verdict.

Ratings are for finding a posting that is confusing. A low one usually means
the description and the form disagree about something.

## The chart

Open a posting for the last 90 days of times opened, apply clicks, and yeses,
with a table of the days something happened on.

Days run midnight to midnight in the timezone the times are stored in, which
the chart says under itself.

A quiet chart on a published posting with a closing date far away usually means
nobody is finding it. That is a tags and title problem before it is anything
else.

## What analytics does not show

**Who.** No names anywhere on this page. The tracking page is where a person
is.

**What they wrote.** That is in the response sheet.

**Unmatched submissions**, which sit at the foot of this page for an admin and
are not in your copy of it.
